### Read the pace_mag row carefully

It passes the criterion as written -- it moved toward truth -- but it covered
only 15% of the distance and still sits several posterior sd from the planted
value. That is not the ordinal head failing. It is a tight expert prior
(sd 0.309) meeting a moderate N: one dimension's `mag`, competing with eleven
other dimension weights and three confounders, gets limited leverage from 400
rows drawn at the lattice resolution the real instruments emit. The binary
head behaves the same way -- `build_model.recovery_curve` needs thousands of
rows before a planted `mag` arrives, which is exactly what that curve exists
to show. Read this run for the parameters the ordinal head ADDS -- the
intercept and the three cutpoint spacings, all recovered inside 2 sd -- and
read the n-needed question off the recovery curve, where it belongs.

What this does and does not establish: the head recovers parameters it was
given, on data it generated itself, at the resolution the real instruments
can emit. It says nothing about whether the expert's weights describe real
placements -- only real outcome data can say that, and E-5's box stays
unchecked until a fit runs on some.
