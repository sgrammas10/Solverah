How the fit-prediction model works (high level, non-technical)

1. We (the experts) write down a starting hypothesis for how each trait
   pairing produces a fit -- e.g. "a high-autonomy person under a
   controlling manager is probably a bad fit." This is written before any
   real user has used the product. It's a best guess, not a measurement.

2. When a person signs up, they take a short quiz that tells us their own
   preferences (e.g. how much autonomy they want). Separately, we have a
   profile of the company/manager they're being matched with. Neither of
   these is a certainty -- they're both "here's what we currently believe,"
   and that belief can be confident or unsure depending on how clear their
   answers were.

3. For any specific person + company pairing, we combine (1) the fixed
   hypothesis with (2) that person's and that company's specific beliefs to
   predict how well they'd fit, how engaged the person would likely be, and
   what guidance to give (e.g. "proceed" vs "reconsider this match").

4. Over time, as we get real outcome data back (how engaged people
   actually turned out to be after being placed), we use that data to
   gradually correct the original hypothesis instead of relying on the
   expert guess forever. Early on, with only a little data, the guess still
   carries most of the weight. As more real outcomes come in, the data
   carries more and more weight, until eventually predictions are driven
   almost entirely by real outcomes rather than the original hypothesis.

Open problem, not yet solved: step 4 depends on being able to observe how
someone is actually doing after they're placed. If people stop using
Solverah once they've found a job, we have no way to collect that
follow-up data. Need a plan for getting a post-placement signal (e.g. a
periodic check-in, or a signal from the employer side) before step 4 is
possible in practice.
