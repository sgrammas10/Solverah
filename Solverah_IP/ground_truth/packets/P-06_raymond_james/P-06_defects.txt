P-06 RAYMOND JAMES — SELF-REPORTED DEFECTS AGAINST COLLECTOR PROMPT v4.6
Build date 2026-08-14 · clean session · one company · assisted: NO

Not fixed, not softened. Classified by the builder. Where genuinely torn, called structural.


================================ STRUCTURAL ================================

S1. ONLY ONE RETRIEVAL METHOD EXISTED IN THIS ENVIRONMENT.
    Prompt section: "YOU NEED TWO RETRIEVAL METHODS, AND YOU MUST CHOOSE BETWEEN THEM PER
    SOURCE" (new in v4.4); defect register D13.
    The JavaScript renderer was not connected. Two attempts returned "Claude in Chrome is not
    connected". Plain HTTP fetch was the only method available for the entire build.
    Why structural: the prompt makes "try the other method" a precondition for recording ANY
    absence, and the whole proof-of-absence apparatus rests on having two. Every not-reached
    finding in this packet is therefore weaker than v4.6 requires, and section A's evidence is
    different evidence from what a two-method build would have produced.
    What the prompt lacks: no instruction for what to do when one method is unavailable. The
    shipping posture says "record the limitation precisely and move on", which is what I did,
    but the prompt does not say whether a single-method build is a valid ground-truth packet
    or a void one. That decision was left to me and it should not have been.

S2. SECTION A'S PRIMARY ROUTE FAILED AND v4.6 STILL SUPPLIES NO FALLBACK CHAIN FOR IT.
    Prompt section: "A. CAREERS / VALUES PAGE"; defect register D12 ("Section A's primary
    route is JavaScript-gated and it had no fallback chain" — logged against P-02, still not
    remedied in v4.6).
    www.raymondjames.com renders its page bodies through JavaScript. Worse, plain fetch of
    those URLs is CUT OFF at roughly 104,700 characters — the site's navigation alone exceeds
    the retrievable length, so the fetch terminates inside the nav before reaching any body.
    Two different careers URLs both terminated at 1,532 and 1,535 lines respectively. This is
    a second, independent failure mode the prompt does not describe anywhere: not a
    "Loading..." shell, not a bot wall, but a length cut-off in the retrieval layer.
    I substituted two other first-party sources — the applicant-tracking careers landing page
    and the board-approved Code of Business Conduct and Ethics PDF. Section C already gives
    section C a five-step fallback ladder; section A gets nothing. I invented one.
    Why structural: it directly changed which evidence reached Section 1's A block.

S3. THE `needed JS: Y/N` FIELD IS UNANSWERABLE WITH ONE METHOD, AND THE PROMPT TREATS IT AS
    UNCONDITIONALLY REQUIRED.
    Prompt section: "Record `needed JS: Y/N` on every excerpt and every entry in the access
    log. This measures what rendering actually buys, per source and per company."
    I recorded "not determinable" on all 47 lines that carry the field. The measurement v4.4
    introduced silently produces a column of nulls rather than an error, so a downstream
    consumer reading the field across packets will see this company as an outlier for a
    reason that has nothing to do with the company.
    Why structural: the field is a per-company measurement the instrument is supposed to
    produce, and this build produces none of it.

S4. RULE 2 DOES NOT SAY WHETHER PAGE METADATA IS "THE OPENED PAGE".
    Prompt section: hard rule 2, "VERBATIM ONLY, FROM THE OPENED PAGE."
    Four of the eight Section A excerpts come from the meta-description element of the careers
    landing page, because the body is JavaScript-generated and did not render. The text is
    verbatim, it is served in that page's HTML, and it is the same marketing copy the page
    body displays. But it is metadata, not rendered body text, and rule 2 was written to bar
    search-engine summaries — which are also, formally, descriptions of a page.
    I decided metadata counts and flagged it. A different builder would defensibly decide it
    does not, and would return a Section A of four excerpts, below the minimum of five.
    Why structural: the decision determines whether section A passes its yield gate.

S5. NO DOUBLE-COUNTING RULE BETWEEN SECTIONS A AND C.
    Prompt sections: "C. JOB POSTINGS"; and the D-section rule "On double-counting. If the
    only narrative text you find at rung 3 is the same careers/values material already quoted
    in section A, it does not count toward D."
    All five postings I opened carry roughly 250 words of IDENTICAL company-wide values
    boilerplate — the five guiding behaviors, the core values list, and a five-bullet "we
    expect our associates at all levels to" block. This is careers-page copy pasted into a job
    posting. The prompt bars A material from counting toward D but says nothing about A
    material counting toward C, and section C's minimum is a count of excerpts.
    I quoted one instance and labelled it as a standing block. Quoting more of it would have
    let me reach C's minimum of six from boilerplate alone, without a single role-specific
    sentence, and nothing in the prompt forbids that.
    Why structural: it is a hole in a required-to-yield gate.

S6. D0's VOICE TAXONOMY HAS NO CATEGORY FOR FIELD MANAGEMENT.
    Prompt section: "D0. VOICE COMPOSITION"; 2a's "___ executive / ___ non-executive".
    My non-executive passages come from a branch and complex manager — a person who manages
    advisors in a field office. He is not on the executive leadership team and not an
    individual contributor. D0 offers a binary.
    I classified him non-executive. That classification is load-bearing: had I called him
    executive, section D would have come back 100% executive and D0 would have obliged me to
    work an additional ladder rung, which would have changed section D's contents.
    Why structural: a coin-flip taxonomy decision gates a mandatory extra search.

S7. B7 ASSUMES TRUNCATED REVIEWS CANNOT BE EXPANDED, AND DOES NOT REQUIRE TESTING THE
    PERMALINK.
    Prompt section: "B7. Truncation is systematic on large companies"; defect register D7.
    40% of my sample is truncated, which triggered the mandatory NOTE TO RATER. But every
    truncated review on this site has an individual permalink, and B7 never says to try it —
    it only says to mark the cut and count it. Earlier prompt versions established that
    individual review permalinks render (v4.3's correction), so the tooling to test this
    already exists in the instrument's own history.
    I did not test the permalinks, to keep the build bounded under the shipping posture. That
    was my decision and I am recording it as one.
    Why structural: if permalinks expand, the length-based selection effect that D7 calls a
    bias running through the whole packet is avoidable, and three packets have now shipped
    with it unnecessarily. If they do not expand, B7 should say so and close the question.

S8. THE PROMPT NEVER SAYS WHETHER TO TAKE MORE THAN 15 REVIEWS WHEN MORE ARE RENDERED.
    Prompt sections: "B. EMPLOYEE REVIEWS — minimum 12 reviews, target 15" versus the OUTPUT
    FORMAT line "B. EMPLOYEE REVIEWS (12–15, one per line, most recent first)".
    One fetch returned 20 reviews, all within the 24-month gate. The section heading states a
    minimum and a target with no maximum; the format block implies a maximum of 15. I took 15
    and discarded 5 that were newer than nothing I kept — i.e. the discard was purely
    positional and cost the packet five in-gate reviews for no stated reason.
    Why structural: it changed the size of the only required-to-yield experiential section,
    and I resolved it by guessing which of two prompt statements governs.


================================ COSMETIC ==================================

C1. SECTION B CONTRADICTS ITSELF ABOUT GLASSDOOR SUBRATINGS.
    v4.5 removed per-title subratings entirely and 2f now prints "NOT COLLECTED". But B2c
    steps 5 and 6 still instruct "open the matching Glassdoor title page for its subrating
    block only" and "Record per title: ... the six subratings", and B3b retains three
    validation checks for rating blocks. B2c is labelled "retained only for the case in B2
    above", which does not obviously cover the subrating steps. Confusing; changed nothing,
    because 2f is unambiguous.

C2. THE OUTPUT FORMAT TEMPLATE HAS NO SLOT FOR `needed JS`.
    The prompt requires the field on every excerpt, but the template line is
    `• "<verbatim quote>" — [source type] — <attribution> — <URL> — <date>`. I appended it as
    a sixth field. Every builder will invent a different position for it.

C3. 2a's YIELD AUDIT RECORDS ONLY MINIMA, NEVER MAXIMA.
    Section A is capped at 8 and section E at 4, both for stated reasons, but the audit block
    has no line for either cap. A packet that over-collects A — the exact failure the cap
    exists to prevent — passes the audit silently.

C4. 2c HAS NO FIELD FOR D0 VOICE COMPOSITION, THOUGH 2a DOES.
    Voice is a per-rung property discovered while working the ladder, and 2c is the per-rung
    block. Recording it only in the summary at 2a means the builder has to hold it in memory
    across seven rungs.

C5. THE REVIEW SITES' COUNT CONTRADICTIONS RECURRED, EXACTLY AS D4 PREDICTS.
    One Indeed page showed 865 / 865 / 933 / 933 simultaneously; one Glassdoor page showed
    2,495 / 2K / 2,971. The prompt already handles this correctly and I followed it. Logged
    as confirmation, not as a prompt fault.

C6. THE STALE-CEO DEFECT RECURRED, EXACTLY AS B3b PREDICTS.
    Glassdoor still names an executive who ceased to be chief executive in February 2025, and
    attaches a CEO-approval percentage to him. The prompt's warning worked — I checked, found
    the current chief executive named in the company's own 2026 code of conduct, and used no
    approval figure. Logged as confirmation.

C7. SECTION F's FIGURE-STRIPPING RULE AND D15's REWRITE RULE PULL AGAINST EACH OTHER ON THE
    SAME PARAGRAPH.
    F says "If a sentence cannot survive removing its figures, leave it out." D15 says
    "REWRITE THE PROPER NOUN, KEEP THE CLAUSE" and explicitly warns against the drop-the-
    excerpt rule in section F. The workforce-composition sentence in this filing is a sentence
    that cannot survive removing its figures AND contains no proper noun. I dropped it, which
    F sanctions. But the two rules sit two paragraphs apart and read as if they govern the
    same decision. Resolvable on a careful read; confusing on a fast one.

C8. RUNG 1 HAS NO GUIDANCE FOR A HANDBOOK THAT IS FOUND BUT UNREADABLE.
    The company's culture "blueprint" — named in both its 10-K and its careers site — exists
    at a first-party URL, fetched successfully, and yielded only marginal call-out
    annotations because the PDF's main text did not extract. That is a fourth distinct state
    alongside the prompt's "does not exist", "stale", and "could not be reached": retrieved,
    confirmed to exist, partially legible. I logged it under its own heading in 2c. The
    prompt's vocabulary does not have a word for it.


================================ NOT DEFECTS, RECORDED ANYWAY ==============

N1. The v4.6 recency frame worked well here and its stated failure mode did not appear.
    Newest review on the two sites differed by two days (2026-07-16 versus 2026-07-14), so
    the P-03/P-04 staleness signature is absent. Four of fifteen reviews carry no job title
    and would have been invisible under the v4.5 title frame — B2a earned its place on this
    company.

N2. No employer responses appeared anywhere in the twenty reviews rendered, so B2b was
    worked and yielded nothing. Recording it because "the rule fired and found nothing" is
    different from "the rule was not worked".
