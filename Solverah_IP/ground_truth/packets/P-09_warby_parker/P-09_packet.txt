########## SECTION 5 — SELF-REPORTED DEFECTS (BUILD_BRIEF step 5) ##########

STRUCTURAL

S-1. The prompt mandates verbatim-only quoting and mandates two retrieval methods, but says
     nothing about a retrieval tool that MEDIATES the page through a language model.
     (Relates to: "REQUIRES A BROWSING TOOL"; hard rule 2 VERBATIM ONLY; the v4.4 two-methods
     rule.) In this environment the plain-fetch method does not return raw page text — it returns
     a small model's answer over the fetched markdown. That is precisely the "search engine's
     result summary is a PARAPHRASE" failure the prompt bars, arriving through the door the
     prompt opened. Structural because it determines whether an excerpt reaching Section 1 is
     verbatim at all, and because roughly a third of this packet's Section 1 lines (B, E, the
     Impact Report passages in D, and F) came through it. I mitigated with double-fetch
     cross-checks and by preferring the renderer's raw DOM wherever the source allowed, and I
     recorded which excerpts carry the residual risk — but the prompt gave me no rule and I
     invented the mitigation. The prompt should either require a raw-text retrieval method or
     require the double-pass cross-check explicitly. THIS IS NOT HYPOTHETICAL ON THIS BUILD: the
     verification pass caught one excerpt in fifteen where the mediated fetch had closed a
     mid-sentence clause with a period that is a comma in the source (see 2e). Without a
     re-check that line would have shipped as a fabricated sentence boundary.

S-2. The v4.7 board-liveness hardening is written only as a condition of claiming the section C
     exemption, so it does not fire for a company that IS hiring — and this build shows the same
     defect biting from the other side. (Relates to: section C, "verify the board you found is
     the company's LIVE one", D24.) Here an indexed Greenhouse board slug for the company is
     dead (404 on both the list API and an indexed posting) while third-party aggregators still
     link to it and the company's real board is on a different ATS entirely. No exemption was
     claimed, so by the letter of v4.7 no liveness check was required — yet a researcher who
     took the fallback chain in its printed order (reviews-site job view, then ATS param, then
     the Greenhouse list API, then board pages, then an aggregator) and reached the aggregator
     rung would have quoted dead postings from a dead board and marked them current. Structural
     because it changes which postings reach Section 1. The check should be unconditional:
     confirm the board via a company-issued apply link BEFORE quoting anything from it, not only
     before accepting a zero.

S-3. D0's trigger is "if section D comes back 100% EXECUTIVE", which does not fire on the case
     this company actually presents. (Relates to: D0 VOICE COMPOSITION.) Section D here is
     1 executive passage and 3 in the company's own corporate voice — 0% non-executive employee,
     but not 100% executive, so the mandatory extra rung is not triggered by the rule as written.
     The harm D0 exists to prevent — a rater reading executive or company description of how the
     work happens as evidence about the shop floor — is fully present, and arguably worse, since
     corporate-voice material has no named speaker at all. Structural because it decides whether
     the extra rung gets worked and whether the rater gets the flag. I worked the extra rung and
     flagged it anyway. The trigger should be "0% non-executive", not "100% executive". Related:
     the prompt's voice taxonomy has only two values (executive / non-executive) and no slot for
     company-authored corporate voice, which is what most of section D turned out to be.

S-4. Rule 9 (the achievability gate) has no defined behaviour when a percentage is printed with
     NO stated n. (Relates to: hard rule 9; B3.) The gate is specified as "achievable at its
     stated n, or it is struck" — but the common real case is a figure with no n at all, and the
     prompt does not say whether that is struck, retained, or retained-and-flagged. It nearly
     mattered here: the Impact Report's engagement percentages appeared without an n on first
     retrieval and only a second, differently-worded fetch surfaced the footnote carrying n=3,250.
     Structural because "strike" and "retain" produce different packets. I chose to keep looking
     for the n rather than strike on sight, and would have struck had none been printed — but
     that was my decision, not the prompt's.

S-5. The two-methods rule assumes the renderer is a superset of plain fetch for first-party
     sites, and this build produced the opposite. (Relates to: the v4.4 retrieval rule; D13.)
     On the company's main careers page, plain fetch returned three body sections ("Who we are",
     "What we do", "Our impact") that the renderer's extraction did not return at all, while the
     renderer returned the exact verbatim text plain fetch could only summarise. D13 records that
     the two methods reach different SOURCES; this is a finer failure — the two methods return
     different CONTENT from the SAME successfully-retrieved page. Structural because a researcher
     who used one method and got a page would reasonably stop, and would silently lose material.
     I ran both on every first-party page as a result; the prompt does not require that.

COSMETIC

C-1. The section D ladder has no rung for a company's annual impact / ESG / CSR report, and it
     is the single richest first-person "how we work" document this company publishes.
     (Relates to: the D ladder, rungs 1–7.) I filed it under rung 3 (culture microsite with
     narrative text) because it is the nearest fit, but it is not a microsite and it is not
     marketing copy in the careers-page sense — it is an annually-published, GRI-benchmarked
     document that describes internal practice. Cosmetic because the material reached Section 1
     either way; it only made the rung log imprecise. A public-company packet will hit this every
