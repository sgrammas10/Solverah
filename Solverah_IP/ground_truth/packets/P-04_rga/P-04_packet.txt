    tool for this — §F's "REWRITE THE PROPER NOUN, KEEP THE CLAUSE" — and then
    explicitly scopes it to Section F ("Do not use the drop-the-excerpt rule
    applied elsewhere"). So Section C is required to use the blunt rule on
    sentences that are structurally identical to the ones Section F is required
    to use the sharp rule on. I dropped the sentences. Real remote-work and
    flexibility evidence was lost from Section 1 as a result.

S5. §D0 — "executive" is never defined, and the definition decides whether the
    extra-rung requirement fires. My D came back 2 C-suite passages and 1
    VP-level function head. If "executive" means C-suite, D is 67% executive and
    no extra rung is required. If it means "management", D is 100% executive and
    one is. I treated VP-level as NON-executive and worked the extra rung
    anyway — which is how the third passage was found — but a builder reading it
    the other way would have stopped at two C-suite quotes and shipped a Section
    D that was entirely an ownership-transition narrative. The header flag the
    prompt requires is also undefined for the mixed case.

S6. §B2c / defect D21 — the prompt now instructs that the job-titles index
    "belongs in 2f" and is "the best artifact for understanding the review
    POPULATION", but gives no instruction for when the index materially
    disagrees with the review stream. Here the index accounts for 24 of 34
    reviews, and two titles that carry rendered reviews and appear in the page's
    OWN "popular job titles" block are missing from the index entirely. On P-01
    this was one omitted title; here it is roughly 30% of the sample. I recorded
    the index, the totals, the shortfall and the label-vs-rendered-title
    mismatches, and used none of it for sampling. But "the best artifact for
    understanding the population" is, on this company, wrong by a third and the
    prompt gives the rater no way to know that unless the builder volunteers it.

COSMETIC — confusing, but changed nothing.

C1. §B2c step 3's URL template. `?fcountry=ALL&start=20` returns an EMPTY
    DOCUMENT on every variant tried, while `?start=20` — which silently drops to
    the US-filtered set — returns page 2 in full. Two requests for the same page,
    one parameter apart, empty versus complete. This is the prompt's own
    meta-pattern ("an empty or implausible response is not an absence") firing
    inside the prompt's own recommended route, and the route is not flagged.

C2. §2f asks for "Newest careers-page modification date". No page on this
    company's site exposes one. The nearest token is a CDN asset version integer.
    The field is unanswerable as written for any site that does not publish
    Last-Modified, which is most of them; the prompt should say what to write.

C3. §B3b instructs "Run three checks on each title" and lists per-title checks —
    but §B/v4.5 removed per-title collection entirely. The rule has no object.
    "Retained for any block you still encounter" does not say what to encounter
    it on now that no title pages are opened.

C4. §"OUTPUT FORMAT — plain text only (no markdown tables, no code blocks, no
    artifacts)" while 2a, 2c, 2f and 2g are themselves supplied as fenced code
    blocks containing a fill-in template. It is unclear whether the ban applies
    to the packet only or to the QA section too, and whether the templates are to
    be reproduced literally. I reproduced their content without the fences.

C5. §E's exclusion list (recruiting-SEO, interview-prep, aggregators, content
    farms, encyclopedias) is silent on trade-press SYNDICATION. The Campaign US
    feature appears verbatim under a second publisher's masthead with a different
    URL. Two URLs, one act of reporting. I counted it once; the prompt does not
    say to.

C6. §C's fallback chain is ordered wrongly for this company, and the
    misordering is instructive. Route 1 (the reviews-site job view) does not just
    fail, it AFFIRMATIVELY STATES "There are no open jobs at R/GA Careers Page
    currently" on the same day eleven roles were open — i.e. route 1 hands you a
    false proof-of-absence for the C exemption. Route 3 (the Greenhouse list API)
    fetched cleanly on the first attempt and answered the open/closed question
    for every posting. Route 3 should be first and route 1 should carry a warning.

C7. Three different recency windows are in force — 24 months (reviews), 18 months
    (section D ladder), 12 months (section E) — none of them named, all referred
    to as recency or staleness. In a packet where the reviews are 25 months old
    and the news is 5 months old, having to hold three unnamed windows is a
    real source of error.

C8. Defect register row D17 lists the impossible-percentage failure as found on
    "P-03, P-04, P-05" and hard rule 9 says the same. On this build the gate
    fired zero times on arithmetic and once on source-currency (the CEO-approval
    figure names a chief executive replaced years ago). The prompt's currency
    check for CEO approval lives in §B3b as a trailing "also ignore…", not in
    the gate, so a builder running only hard rule 9 would have passed a figure
    that should be struck.
