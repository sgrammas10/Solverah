########## SECTION 5 - DEFECT REPORT ON PROMPT v4.6 (NEVER IN THE PACKET) ##########

Ten defects. Six structural, four cosmetic. Structural first.


--- STRUCTURAL ---

S1. SECTION B HAS NO BEHAVIOUR FOR "THE ONLY REQUIRED REVIEW SOURCE IS EMPTY."
    Prompt section: B1, and the FINAL CHECK line "Did I take review TEXT from
    Indeed, and keep Glassdoor's Pros/Cons fragments out of Section 1 entirely?"
    v4.5 made Indeed the only required review source and demoted Glassdoor to an
    optional recency cross-check whose bodies "may never" be quoted into Section
    1 "in any case". That pairing is safe only while Indeed is non-empty. Here
    Indeed holds zero reviews under both entities while Glassdoor holds 29, so
    the rule as written produces a packet with NO non-company-authored
    experiential evidence about a company for which such evidence demonstrably
    exists and is publicly readable. I followed the rule literally, because "in
    any case" admits no reading that lets me override it, and recorded the
    consequence in a NOTE TO RATER. This is the largest single effect on what
    reached Section 1 in this build. The v4.5 reasoning for the bar - that
    Glassdoor renders Pros/Cons fragments rather than prose - did not hold on the
    page I actually opened: the newest review's Cons block rendered as eight full
    sentences, and the two adjacent reviews rendered short prose. The evidence
    for the bar is a large-company rendering property that does not reproduce at
    n=29. A fallback clause is needed: something like "if the required source
    returns zero reviews after a completed entity check, the cross-check source
    becomes the text source, and every excerpt taken from it is tagged with the
    source name so the rater can discount the format."

S2. THE BUILD RAN WITH ONE RETRIEVAL METHOD, NOT TWO.
    Prompt section: the "YOU NEED TWO RETRIEVAL METHODS" block, and defect D13.
    The JavaScript renderer was not connected in this environment and returned a
    not-connected error on every attempt. Plain fetch alone resolved 27 of 29
    URLs, so most of the packet is unaffected - but two states are unresolved
    that a renderer would very likely have settled: the Ashby board, which
    returned a generic JS shell that renders identically for valid and invalid
    slugs, and Glassdoor review page 2, which returned an empty document twice.
    Neither is recorded as an absence. Structural because a renderer might have
    changed section C's exemption basis and would have changed how much of the
    Glassdoor review population I could characterise in 2f. The prompt tells the
    researcher to use two methods but has no instruction for what to do when the
    environment supplies only one - it should say explicitly that the packet
    proceeds, that every unresolved state is recorded as unresolved rather than
    absent, and that the missing method is stamped in the build record.

S3. THE INVERSE OF DEFECT D13 OCCURRED AND THE PROMPT PREDICTS THE OPPOSITE.
    Prompt section: the two-retrieval-methods block ("First-party sites - careers,
    values, culture, handbooks, blogs - are frequently JavaScript single-page
    apps... Use the renderer") and defect D12.
    Every first-party page here - careers, about-us, how-we-work, press, the
    engineering blog, the culture newsletter - returned in full to plain fetch.
    Zero of 27 successful retrievals needed JS. The pages plain fetch could not
    resolve were an ATS board and an aggregator pagination route, which is the
    reverse of the routing rule. A researcher who followed the routing guidance
    as written would have reached for the renderer first on section A and, in an
    environment where it is unavailable, might have recorded a first-party
    absence that does not exist. Structural because the routing rule determines
    which sources get a second attempt before being written off. The rule should
    be restated as a per-source empirical test rather than a per-class
    prediction: try one, and if the response is empty or a shell, try the other,
    regardless of which class the source belongs to.

S4. SECTION A'S CAP OF 8 HAS NO SELECTION RULE, SO THE CAP IS EXERCISED BY
    RESEARCHER TASTE.
    Prompt section: A ("minimum 5, maximum 8 excerpts").
    This company's own pages yielded at least eleven quotable, clearly
    dimension-bearing sentences. Rule 6 forbids selecting on content for review
    sources and says selecting on content "biases the packet in a way nobody
    downstream can detect or correct" - but section A imposes a hard cap with no
    ordering rule at all, which forces exactly that selection and gives it no

    marathon, not a sprint" and the entire formula-based compensation and hiring

    definition - it changed what evidence reached Section 1. Needs a deterministic
    rule (document order, or one per dimension then document order to the cap)
    and a line in 2f recording what was dropped.

S5. B2b SAYS TO COLLECT EMPLOYER RESPONSES; B1 SAYS NEVER TO QUOTE GLASSDOOR INTO
