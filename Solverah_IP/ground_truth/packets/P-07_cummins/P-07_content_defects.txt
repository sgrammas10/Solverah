CD-1  Section E header undercounts its own section.
      Header reads "--- E. RECENT NEWS (yield: 2 excerpts · 4 searches logged) ---".
      The section contains FOUR excerpt bullets: two from the joint-venture pause story and two
      from the vice-president appointments story. The "4 searches logged" figure is consistent
      with section 2a; the "2 excerpts" figure is not, and section 2a itself records
      "E excerpts: 4 (2 events)". The likely origin is that the header counts EVENTS and the
      label says excerpts. Header reproduced verbatim; neither number corrected.
      Impact: a rater or a harness reading the declared yield sees half the section.

CD-2  A review headline contradicts its own review body.
      rev-04 (2026-07-22, Support Specialist, [Site C], the entry the source site renders three
      times) carries a positive headline about the company being good to work for. The body is
      uniformly negative across all four of its clauses: an inexperienced replacement manager,
      union representation described as useless, shop stewards dismissed, and a further negative
      clause cut off by source truncation. The headline is the reviewer's own text and is kept
      verbatim under A7 — the contradiction is itself evidence, and it only exists because the
      headline is retained — but a rater skimming headlines will read this voice backwards.
      Because the source site duplicates this entry, the same contradiction appears three times
      in section B and could be mistaken for a pattern across three voices.

CD-3  Section B's rater-facing scope declaration names role types that do not appear in the
      section.
      The scope declaration at the head of the packet says the reviews come from plant,
      distribution and field-service roles "plus a small number of coordinator, analyst and
      specialist roles". Specialist is present (Support Specialist, and arguably Technical
      Support). NO coordinator and NO analyst title appears anywhere in the 15 reviews. The full
      sampled title list is: two entries with no title stated, Material Handler, Team Lead,
      Support Specialist (x3 entries, one voice), Credit Assistant, Forklift Operator/Material
      Handler, Temporary Employee, Production Associate (x2), Service Writer, Line Technician,
      Manufacturing Project Lead, Cross Functional Engineering Associate, Technical Support.
      The only coordinator in the packet is in section D, which is company-published, not a
      review. Section 2g additionally lists "one order-management specialist, one disability
      specialist" in the B population; neither title appears in the 15 sampled reviews either.
      Impact: the scope declaration is the block a rater uses to decide who the reviews speak
      for, and it currently claims a slightly broader and more white-collar sample than exists.

CD-4  Section E excerpt 3 is tagged as news reporting but is a company press release.
      Its attribution credits the text to the company ("Courtesy-" the company name) in the raw
      packet, i.e. the outlet reprinted a company release. It is tagged "[news reporting]".
      Excerpt 4, taken from the SAME page and the same release, is correctly tagged
      "[company statement quoted inside news reporting]". So one release yields two excerpts
      with two different source classes.
      Impact: this is not cosmetic. The source class is load-bearing — the proposed 0.7 rule
      turns on company-authored versus not — and excerpt 3 will be counted as non-company-
      authored corroboration when it is company-authored. Both excerpts reproduced as built;
      the class tags were not changed.

      The note tells the rater that "the source URLs, which Rule 3 requires, themselves contain
