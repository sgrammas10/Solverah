########## SECTION 5 — BUILDER'S DEFECT REPORT ON THE COLLECTOR PROMPT ##########

Prompt under test: Company_Culture_Evidence_Collector_v4.7. Company: Twilio (P-10).

--- STRUCTURAL (changed what evidence reached Section 1, or how a section was scoped) ---

S1. SECTION B / B1 / B1a-REMEDY — THE STALENESS DETECTOR MEASURES THE WRONG THING WHEN THE
    REQUIRED SOURCE IS SMALL BUT TOPPED UP.
    v4.7's remedy fires on the age of the NEWEST review. Here the required source's newest
    review is 2026-08-08 and the optional source's is 2026-08-24 — sixteen days apart — so
    both B1a conditions fail and the prompt's verdict is "no problem". Underneath that single
    current review the required source holds 62 reviews in total against roughly 2,095 on the
    optional source for the same employer (about 34x), nine of my fifteen sit outside the
    24-month gate, and the median review age is about 28 months. This is D22's failure with
    the recency signature filed off: one recent post at the top of a tiny, old population
    defeats a detector that only looks at the top. Consequence for this packet: Section B had
    to be filled down to 2023-11-17 while several hundred in-gate reviews sat on the optional
    source, and no rule in v4.7 permitted me to touch them. Suggested test to add: fire the
    remedy on POPULATION as well as recency — e.g. when the optional source holds more than
    ~5x the required source's total, or when the required source's median review age exceeds
    24 months, regardless of the newest date.

S2. HARD RULE 9 / B3 — THE ACHIEVABILITY GATE IS UNDEFINED WHEN NO n IS PRINTED.
    The gate says a percentage "must be reachable at the sample size printed beside it." It
    says nothing about the far more common case where no n is printed at all. The company's
    culture page prints "95% of Twilions report effective team collaboration in remote work"
    with no n, no denominator and no methodology anywhere on the page. I STRUCK it, reasoning
    that a figure that cannot be shown achievable has not passed a gate it never faced. A
    builder reading the same rule could equally have kept it with a footnote, since the rule's
    strike condition ("if it is not [achievable]") is never actually met. That is a coin-flip
    deciding whether a piece of company-authored evidence enters Section 1. Structural because
    it changed Section 1's contents, and because I am genuinely torn, which the prompt says to
    resolve as structural.

S3. SECTION B / B0 — NO RULE FOR ACQUIRED BRANDS THAT KEEP THEIR OWN REVIEW PROFILE.
    B0 tells me to find subsidiaries and to "collect from all entities, newest-first across the
    merged set." Twilio's acquired brand SendGrid holds its own profile with 11 reviews. Every
    one of those reviews was written about a company that was independently run at the time, in
    a different city, under different management. B0 as written would have merged them straight
    into Section B on date order alone. It was inert here only by luck — SendGrid's newest
    review is 2020-04-29, older than all fifteen I took, so the merged sample is identical. Had
    the acquired brand been more recently acquired or more actively reviewed, the prompt would
    have required me to put another company's working conditions into this packet. Neither B0
    nor the v4.7 pooled-homonym extension covers this: the homonym rule is about a DIFFERENT
    company with the same name; this is the SAME corporate family describing a different
    workplace. Suggested rule: merge an acquired brand's reviews only where they postdate the
    acquisition close, and record the close date in 2f.

S4. SECTION D / SECTION E — NO RULE ON ONE SOURCE SERVING TWO SECTIONS.
    The prompt has an explicit double-counting rule for A vs. D and nothing for D vs. E. The
    Fortune profile (2026-05-04) satisfies ladder rung 6 AND is in-window reporting for E. I
    routed it to E only, which left D at 3 passages with a 2 non-executive / 1 executive voice
    composition. Routing it to both would have given D 4 passages at 2/2, changing the voice
    composition figure that D0 exists to surface — and voice composition is exactly the field
    v4.5 added because it silently tracks stage_size. A discretionary choice should not move a
    confounder-detection field. Structural.

S5. PARITY / "NEVER SIGN IN" — THE RULE HAS NO VERIFICATION STEP, AND THE RENDERER MAY BE
    SOMEONE ELSE'S LOGGED-IN BROWSER.
    The prompt bars authenticated content because production scrapes anonymously, and it
    assumes the researcher decides whether a session is authenticated. In this environment the
    "JavaScript renderer" of v4.4 is the operator's own browser, carrying whatever sessions the
    operator happens to have. On any site where the operator is signed in, the renderer would
    return authenticated content silently, and the packet would be richer than production can
    reproduce with nothing in the output to show it — the parity violation running backwards,
    exactly as the prompt describes, but arriving through the tool rather than through a
    decision. It did not bite here: the renderer was Cloudflare-walled on both review sites, and
    the first-party pages I rendered carry no login. But the prompt requires no check. Suggested
    addition: before using the renderer on any source, confirm and record that the session is
    anonymous, and record it per-source in the access log alongside `needed JS`.

S6. SECTION A — A CLIENT-SIDE TAB CONTROL HIDES THREE QUARTERS OF THE PRIMARY SOURCE AFTER
    RENDERING SUCCEEDS.
    D12 records that Section A's primary route is JavaScript-gated. It does not record that
    successful rendering is not the end of it. The values page renders four value headings and
    then a tab strip; on load, only the "We are builders" panel exists in the DOM. The other
    three values' principles — which is where "Disagree and commit", "Share problems, not
    solutions", "Seek progress over perfection" and "Be humble" live — appear only after a
    click, and only then does the URL expose itself as ?tab=1_2 / 1_3 / 1_4. Plain fetch, the
    renderer's text extraction, and a naive innerText read ALL return the same one-quarter view,
    with no gap and no error. Five of my eight Section A excerpts, and every excerpt bearing on
    FEEDBACK, sit behind that click. This is a seventh variant of the register's meta-pattern —
    a complete-looking response is not a complete response — and it is the first one where the
    page is not empty, not walled and not stale, just partial. Structural, and I would put it in
    the defect register: on any first-party values or careers page, enumerate tab, accordion and
    "read more" controls and open each before recording Section A as collected.

S7. SECTION A — THE 8-EXCERPT CAP COLLIDES WITH RULE 2'S TYPO-PRESERVATION DUTY.
    Rule 2 requires source typos to be reproduced and marked, and Section 4 requires them
    flagged as unique-string identifiers. The values page's closing sentence reads "when you act
    as a builder, an owner, a position, and lead with curiosity" — "a position" where the same

    packet's most distinctive identifying string. I recorded it in 2e and Section 4 without
    quoting it. The prompt does not say whether an excerpt retained solely for typo-preservation
    counts against the cap. Structural because the cap decided what reached Section 1.
