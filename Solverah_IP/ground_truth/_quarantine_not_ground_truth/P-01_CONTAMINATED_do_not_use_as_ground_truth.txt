     ** ALL 20 fall inside the 24-month gate. **

The company is NOT stale. The TITLE FILTER is stale. Three mechanisms, all visible in the data:

  1. **Recent reviews are frequently posted with NO job title at all.** Three of the twenty most
     recent render as "Anonymous". A review with no title belongs to no bucket, is invisible to
     any title-stratified sample, and cannot be scoped in 2g either.

  2. **Recent reviews use long-tail title strings that do not map to the index's buckets** —
     "Insurance Operations Claims Processor", "Complex Case Manager", "Clinical Coordinator",
     "UM", "Care guide" (lower case), "Customer Service", "Insurance Operations Supervisor".
     The index normalises to "Claims Processor", "Case Manager", "Customer Service
     Representative". The normalisation is what strands them.

  3. **The large normalised buckets are historical.** Member Services Representative was a mass
     hiring role in this company's 2016–2021 era. The bucket is large *because* it is old.

**Consequence: a v4.5-conformant Section B would describe Oscar Health circa 2016–2018.** The
company-wide stream describes 2025–2026 — layoffs, remote work, micromanagement, a nurse-scope
regulatory concern, "no longer any transparency", "the tech org". Two different companies.

This is a STRUCTURAL defect by the freeze rule's own definition: it changes what evidence reaches
Section 1. **v4.5 does not freeze.**

### Why this did not appear on P-02, P-03, P-04 or P-05

P-02 had 39,859 reviews against 85,000 employees — every bucket was continuously replenished, so
newest-within-title and newest-company-wide agreed. P-03 and P-04 are mid-size with concentrated
functions. Oscar is ~2,305 employees with 237 reviews, a shrinking headcount, and a role mix that
has turned over since the reviews were written. **The title frame works when review volume is high
and the role mix is stable. It fails when either is false, and it fails silently — the buckets look
fine, the counts agree, and the dates are the only tell.**

### THE DECISION REQUIRED BEFORE SECTION B CAN BE BUILT

Not a workaround — a rule. Options as I see them:

  (a) **Recency-first frame with title recorded per review.** Sample company-wide newest-first,
      record each review's title as metadata, and report the resulting function mix in 2g rather
      than imposing one. Keeps dispersion visible, keeps the sample current, loses the guarantee
      of function coverage. **My recommendation.**
  (b) **Keep the title frame and mark P-01 Section B as FAIL on recency.** Honest, and produces a
      packet a rater cannot score on experiential evidence.
  (c) **Hybrid:** title-stratified where a bucket has ≥N reviews inside the gate, company-wide
      newest-first for the remainder. More faithful, and a second frame to specify and maintain.

Whichever is chosen, two sub-rules are needed regardless:
  * **Untitled ("Anonymous") reviews need a rule.** They are currently uncollectable and unscopeable.
  * **Employer RESPONSES embedded in the review stream need a tag.** One is present here, dated
    2021-02-22, and it is company-authored content sitting inside an experiential source. It is
    dimension-bearing and must never be tagged `[employee review]`.
