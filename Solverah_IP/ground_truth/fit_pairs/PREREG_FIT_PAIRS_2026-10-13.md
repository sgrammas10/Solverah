## 2. Design

**Unit.** A *pair* is one fictional person and one fictional company read. A *block* is one
person with five companies. The model's output is an order over companies **for one
person**, so every comparison happens inside a block, and a rater who stops after any block
leaves only complete blocks. There are 10 blocks, 50 pairs and 4 repeats, so 54 screens. At
about a minute a screen, that is roughly 6 to 8 minutes per block, and about an hour in all.

**The person** is shown in plain words, never numbers: a preference per trait (five levels,
"Strongly prefers Steady" to "Strongly prefers Fast"); a tolerance per trait and direction
("Faster: a little is OK, a lot is not"), which is `under_steps`/`over_steps` 0/1/2 as stored
by `trait_preferences`; and up to two must-haves.

**The company** is shown as one read per trait. A known read uses five levels ("Clearly
Steady" to "Clearly Fast") and gives one paraphrased line of independent evidence. A
mixed read shows both ends, the reason for the split (scope, time or none) and one line
per side. An unknown read says "Not enough evidence found". A coverage line gives counts,
and no model verdict, flag or next step is shown. Every company value sits **exactly on a
band** (0.1 to 0.9), so each word on the page maps one to one onto the number the model
scores. Headcount and confounders are empty for every company, so nothing but the six reads
differs inside a block.

**The question**, framed like the `experienced` item: *"If {name} took a job at this
company, how well do you think it would work for them?"* The answers are 1 "It wouldn't work
at all", 2 "It wouldn't work well", 3 "A mixed picture", 4 "It would work well", 5 "It would
work very well", plus **"Can't say from what's here."** (an abstention, stored as
`rating: null, abstain: true`). There is an optional one-line note.

**Cases** (where the model is most opinionated). `design_check` in the spec restates the
weight-free gate rules per pair, and the tests prove each case is what it says.

| block | person | companies (case) |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | Rosa: must-have autonomy | aligned; must-have break; three big gaps; 3 unknown; gaps inside wide tolerance |
| B2 | Tom: must-haves pace, feedback | aligned; must-have mixed (scope); one-notch must-have break; four gaps inside tolerance; one big gap |
| B3 | Lena: zero tolerance, no must-haves | aligned; six small gaps; one big gap; two medium gaps; 2 known only (unranked) |
| B4 | Marcus: zero tolerance | aligned; compound 1 (less say + faster); its mirror; same-size control pair; pace gap alone |
| B5 | Ade: zero tolerance, must-have innovation | aligned; compound 2 (faster + blunter); compound 4 (more collective + blunter); same-size control; must-have unknown |
| B6 | Jun: must-haves feedback, collaboration | aligned; compound 3 (steadier + more refining); its mirror; must-have mixed (time); wide mixed, non-must-have |
| B7 | Sam: asymmetric tolerance, must-have innovation | aligned; big gaps in the tolerated direction; the same gaps mirrored into the untolerated direction; 3 unknown incl. the must-have; mixed with both ends inside tolerance |
| B8 | Nadia: must-haves collaboration, decision | aligned; must-have break; three big gaps; three small gaps; one big gap |
| B9 | Ines: zero tolerance on autonomy, pace, innovation | aligned; pace gap (faster); innovation gap (more inventing); innovation gap (more refining); autonomy gap (control) |
| B10 | Kofi: as B9, must-have feedback (always met) | aligned; pace gap (steadier); innovation gap (more refining); innovation gap (more inventing); autonomy gap (control) |

**B9 and B10 (added 2026-10-14, before any data)** follow `match_model_alignment/07_SENSITIVITY_RESULTS.md`.
Of the twelve weights, only pace size, innovation size and innovation direction (feedback size
borderline) move rankings. In these two blocks every gap is the same distance (two notches,
0.4) outside the same zero tolerance on ONE trait, so a pair of companies differs in which
trait carries the gap, or in which direction, and nothing else. The control is autonomy, the
smallest penalty in `priors.yaml`. Pace is varied in both directions across the blocks;
innovation is varied in both directions inside each block.

The compounding pairs are 06 items 1-4. Each is placed against a **same-size control** in the
same block: the same distances outside tolerance, on the mirrored directions or on traits
outside any 06 pair. `test_compound_and_controls_have_equal_gap_sizes` pins this.

**Repeats.** R1 = B1-C4 (3 unknown), R2 = B2-C2 (must-have mixed), R3 = B3-C2 (six small
gaps) and R4 = B4-C2 (compound 1). Each is shown again, unannounced, at the end of the part
two parts later, so a rater who stops after block k still has every repeat up to k. **Each
repeat is the same question by construction.** It references the same pair id, and its card
is rendered by the same function from the same objects, so it is byte-identical (tested in
Python and in jsdom). It is not reworded. The priors form's re-ask turned a *need* into a
*tolerance* and asked a different question. The price of identity is recognisability, so
repeat agreement is an **upper bound** on a rater's consistency.

**Order.** Every rater sees the blocks in the same fixed order, B1, B9, B2, B10, B3, B4, B5,
B6, B7, B8, so overlap between raters is a prefix. B9 and B10 come second and fourth so
that a rater who stops at the 4-block minimum has done both. Companies are shuffled inside each block, seeded by
(design version, rater, block), and the page records the order.

## 3. The hook: what matching-model scores (not generated here)

This lane does **not** produce model rankings. matching-model scores `pair_specs.json` with
the serving path and writes `pair_model_scores.json` beside it.

### 3a. Input: `pair_specs.json` (`schema: "fit_pairs_spec_v1"`)

```json
{
  "schema": "fit_pairs_spec_v1",
  "design_version": "fp-v1-2026-10-13",
  "generated_by": "Solverah_IP/ground_truth/fit_pairs/design.py",
  "trait_keys": ["autonomy","pace","collaboration","feedback","innovation","decision"],
  "conventions": { "...": "plain-text restatement of the rules below" },
  "persons": [{
    "person_id": "P1", "name": "Rosa",
    "scores":    {"<trait>": 0 | 0.25 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1.0},
    "tolerance": {"<trait>": {"under_steps": 0|1|2, "over_steps": 0|1|2}},
    "must_haves": ["<trait>", ...]                       // 0..2
  }],
  "companies": [{
    "company_id": "B1-C1", "headcount": null, "confounders": {},
    "traits": {"<trait>":
        {"status": "known",   "estimate": 0.1|0.3|0.5|0.7|0.9, "evidence": [...]}
      | {"status": "mixed",   "low_end": <band>, "high_end": <band>,
         "split_explained_by": "scope"|"time"|null, "recent_side": "low"|"high" (time only),
         "evidence": [...]}
      | {"status": "unknown", "evidence": []}}
  }],
  "blocks": [{"block_id": "B1", "person_id": "P1", "pair_ids": ["B1-C1", ...]}],
  "pairs":  [{"pair_id": "B1-C1", "block": "B1", "person_id": "P1", "company_id": "B1-C1",
              "case": "aligned", "design_check": {...}}],
  "repeats":   [{"repeat_id": "R1", "of_pair": "B1-C4", "shown_after_block": "B3"}],
  "contrasts": [{"contrast_id": "K1", "a": [...], "b": [...], "rule": "tie"|"b_last"|"weights", ...}]
}
```

How to score it, so that the result is the product's own ranking:
- **Person.** `scores` are core dimension scores. Zones come from `trait_preferences.flat_zones`
  on `TraitPreference(under_steps, over_steps, must_have, answered=True)`. Must-haves are the
  listed traits.
- **Company.** Each trait is a company-scope trait record with the given status and values.
  `headcount` is None, so `stage_size` is missing, and confounders are all missing. They are
  identical across a block and cannot change a within-block order.
- **Ranking.** Use the same `_trait_read` → `_worst_case_z` → `_order` path as
  `company_match_service`, with weights from `priors.yaml` (`SOLVERAH_MODEL_WEIGHTS_SOURCE`
  unset). Score each block on its own: one person against that block's five companies.
- `evidence` and `design_check` are for pages and cross-checks. **The model must not read
  `design_check`.**
- Score `pair_specs.json` byte-for-byte as committed, and put its sha256 in the output.

### 3b. Output: `pair_model_scores.json` (`schema: "fit_pairs_model_scores_v1"`)

```json
{
  "schema": "fit_pairs_model_scores_v1",
  "spec_sha256": "<sha256 of pair_specs.json bytes>",
  "model": {"weights_source": "priors", "priors_sha256": "...", "model_run_id": null,
            "git_sha": "...", "scored_at": "ISO-8601"},
  "pairs": [{
    "pair_id": "B1-C1",
    "state": "ranked" | "not_enough_evidence",
    "z": <float> | null,                  // worst-case z; null when not ranked
    "must_have_break": <bool>,
    "risk_flag": <bool>,
    "next_step": "decide" | "investigate" | "explore",
    "rank_in_block": <int> | null         // the service's own _order, for cross-checking only
  }]
}
```

The analysis orders ranked pairs by `(not must_have_break, z)` and keeps **exact z ties as
ties** (z values within 1e-9). It never uses `rank_in_block` as the order, because the
service breaks z ties by company id, which would invent an order the model does not hold.
z, like everything in this folder, never reaches a user.

## 4. Pre-registered contrasts

Each contrast compares pair *a* with pair *b* inside a block. **Rule** is what the model's
weight-free rules imply: **tie** means z is equal by construction (nothing outside the zone,
unknown costs nothing); **b_last** means the gate sorts *b* below every non-break company;
**weights** means the direction comes from `priors.yaml` and is read from the model output.

| id | contrast | instances (a vs b) | rule | default at stake |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | must-have break vs several big gaps | B1-C3/C2, B8-C3/C2 | b_last | lexicographic must-have sort |
| K2 | a one-notch must-have break vs one big gap, and vs a split must-have | B2-C5/C3, B2-C2/C3 | b_last | break sorts last however small |
| K3 | unknown traits cost nothing | B1-C1/C4, B7-C1/C4, B5-C1/C5 | tie | unknown = no gap, no penalty |
| K4 | a gap inside stated tolerance costs nothing | B1-C1/C5, B2-C1/C4, B7-C1/C2 | tie | the flat zone (hinge) |
| K5 | many small gaps vs one big gap | B3-C2/C3, B8-C4/C5 | weights | linear excess, summed |
| K6 | same gaps, tolerated vs untolerated direction | B7-C2/C3 | weights | asymmetric tolerance |
| K7 | split evidence with both ends inside tolerance | B7-C1/C5 | tie | mixed scored at its worst end, no penalty for disagreement |
| K8 | compounding pairs vs same-size controls | B4-C3/C2, B4-C4/C2, B5-C4/C2, B5-C4/C3, B6-C3/C2 | weights | gaps add; compounding ignored (06) |
| K9 | a pace gap vs the same gap on autonomy | B9-C5/C2, B10-C5/C2 | weights | pace.mag, the largest penalty (07) |
| K10 | an innovation gap (each direction) vs the same gap on autonomy | B9-C5/C3, B10-C5/C3, B9-C5/C4, B10-C5/C4 | weights | innovation.mag (07) |
| K11 | job more inventing than wanted vs job more refining than wanted | B9-C3/C4, B10-C4/C3 | weights | innovation.dir = 0, elicited 5 v 5 (07) |

Under today's `priors.yaml`, K11 is a model tie (innovation.dir = 0). The contrast fails if the
raters break that tie the same way in both instances (`a` is always the more-inventing side).
That is the same question as Julie's ask 08, question 6, answered by independent readers.
For K9 and K10 a failure means the raters see the pace (or innovation) gap as no worse than
the autonomy gap, while the model costs it more: evidence that the size is too large.

B3-C5 (two known traits) is **unranked** by the model. It is reported on its own: the
abstention rate, and its rating distribution against B3's aligned pair. It is never part of tau.

## 5. Order of operations (each step can stop the run)

0. **Cross-check the model against the spec.** State, `must_have_break` and `risk_flag` must
   equal `design_check`. Every **tie** contrast must be a model tie, and every **b_last**
   contrast must order as stated. A mismatch is a defect in the spec or the scoring adapter
   (test the check before the data): STOP and fix it, and read no rater before that.
1. **Eligibility.** Refuse excluded raters (section 1). Count raters with 4 or more
   complete blocks.
2. **Agreement between raters, first.** Compute the stratified Kendall tau-b inside blocks
   for every pair of counted raters, on first showings only, and take the mean.
3. **Raters vs the model.** Compute the stratified tau-b between each rater's ratings and the
   model order (ranked pairs, first showings), and the same for the **consensus**: the median
   rating per pair over counted raters, kept only where at least 2 raters did not abstain.
4. **Contrasts (section 4) and repeats (section 7).**

**Stratified tau-b** = sum over blocks of (concordant - discordant) /
sqrt(sum of pairs untied on the rating x sum of pairs untied on the model). Pairs from
different blocks are never compared. Abstentions drop out pairwise.

## 6. Decision rules (thresholds fixed now)

| condition | verdict |
|---|---|
| step 0 fails | **STOP**: fix spec/adapter |
| fewer than 3 counted raters | **DESCRIPTIVE ONLY**: report numbers, decide nothing |
| mean inter-rater tau-b < 0.40 | **NO CONCLUSION ABOUT THE MODEL**: raters disagree with each other; defaults stand by default, not by evidence |
| consensus tau-b < 0.20 | **OPEN A WEIGHTS REVIEW** |
| 0.20 <= consensus tau-b < 0.50, or any contrast fails | **EXAMINE THE FAILED CONTRASTS** |
| consensus tau-b >= 0.50 and no contrast fails | **DEFAULTS STAND** |

**A contrast fails** when, in **every** instance, both of these hold (and, for model ties,
the raters break the tie toward the same side every time). First, a majority of the
counted raters with both ratings order the two pairs against the model. For a model tie,
"against" means a majority strictly prefer the same side. Second, the consensus agrees with
that majority. A failed contrast names the default it puts at stake (section 4). That default
is what a change would touch: for example, a soft must-have penalty instead of sorting last,
a cost for unknown traits, a partial slope inside tolerance, or a compounding term.

**No result changes anything automatically.** Every verdict other than "defaults stand" opens
a pre-registration conversation with matching-model and Sebastian, using the evidence above.
The sensitivity test (07) shows whether the weight in question even moves rankings. Any
change is written into `priors.yaml` with its why and date, through matching-model.

**Sizing** (`power_sim.py`, 400 sims per cell). The generative model is stated in its
docstring. rho is the raters' shared agreement with the model. "stand" is P(gate passes and
tau >= 0.50) and "review" is P(gate passes and tau < 0.20).

| blocks | raters | rater noise | rho 0.9 stand / review | rho 0.7 | rho 0.5 | rho 0.3 stand / review | gate passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 3 | 0.5 | 0.97 / 0.00 | 0.55 / 0.01 | 0.14 / 0.12 | 0.02 / 0.50 | 0.99 |
| 8 | 3 | 0.8 | 0.73 / 0.00 | 0.35 / 0.01 | 0.08 / 0.12 | 0.01 / 0.36 | 0.73-0.77 |
| 4 | 3 | 0.5 | 0.90 / 0.00 | 0.55 / 0.04 | 0.20 / 0.21 | 0.06 / 0.48 | 0.96 |
| 10 | 3 | 0.5 | 0.98 / 0.00 | 0.62 / 0.01 | 0.10 / 0.10 | 0.01 / 0.46 | 1.00 |

At 8 blocks and 3 raters, a model the raters broadly share (rho 0.9) is never sent to review,
and a model they do not share (rho 0.3) almost never stands (2%). The review trigger sits at
the tau a rho of 0.3 implies, so it fires about half the time there. A middling model lands
in "examine". The design was sized at 8 blocks. Two blocks were then added (B9, B10, for the
sensitivity weights), so the full task is 10 blocks; the 10-block row is what a finishing
rater buys.
**Half-finished is valid.** At 4 blocks, false review stays at 0 and a bad model stands
at most 6% of the time, so a rater who stops at the midpoint still counts. More raters
mainly buy the agreement gate. Noisier raters (sigma 0.8) fail the gate about a quarter of
the time, which is the intended outcome when raters cannot agree.

## 7. Repeats

For each rater, report n, exact agreement, agreement within 1 point and the maximum
difference. A rater with **two or more repeats differing by 2+ points** is flagged. The
primary analysis keeps them, and a sensitivity analysis drops flagged raters and reports
whether the verdict changes. Because repeats are identical, these figures are upper bounds.

## 8. Abstentions and partial returns

- Abstention is an answer, not missing data. It drops out pairwise and is reported per
  rater and per case. A case type with abstention above 50% across raters is reported as
  "raters could not judge this", and its contrast is not judged.
- A partial return counts toward the verdict if it covers 4 or more complete blocks.
  Incomplete blocks still count in stratified tau (they hold fewer pairs).
- Rater overlap is a prefix (fixed block order). Report the number of raters per block.

## 9. What this does not show

- These are judgements about **fictional** pairs from people who are not the job seekers. A
  PASS means "the model orders fit the way independent readers do"; it is not outcome
  validity. Only steps (4) and (5) in PRODUCT_DECISIONS can show that.
- The page's words for tolerance ("a little" = one notch, "a lot" = two) are our best-guess
  translation of the product's steps. A rater who reads "a little" differently is measuring
  the wording too.
- Pairs hold everything outside the six traits fixed by instruction. Real choices do not.
- Raters see the company read **as the pipeline would report it**. This tests the gate and
  the weights, not the research or the aggregation (that is the concordance study and
  Julie's ask 08).
- One expert's weights against a handful of unpaid raters: agreement among a few
  non-experts is a weak standard. That is why the gate checks raters against each other
  first.

  - At 4 blocks with R = 2, rho 0.9 stands 0.87 of the time, and rho 0.3 stands 0.10 (0.06
    at R = 3).
  - With noisier raters (sigma 0.8) and R = 2, the gate passes only 0.64-0.77 of the time,
    and rho 0.9 stands 0.58-0.70.
  - Read the R = 2 rows as what the excluding-D numbers would mean if they were allowed to
    decide. This amendment does not lower the floor.
- **The gate tests rater noise, not the model.** In the simulation the raters share one
  truth, so the gate passes at about the same rate for every rho (inter-rater tau-b is about
  0.65 at sigma 0.5). Two raters who agree with each other and both disagree with the model
  pass the gate. Catching that is the job of the consensus tau-b and the contrasts.
- **With 2 raters the gate is a single tau-b.** It is one pair, not a mean over pairs. The
  consensus is the median of two ratings, which is their mean (half-points are possible).
  A pair enters the consensus only if **both** raters rated it, so either rater's abstention
  drops it. A contrast "majority" means both raters.
- **The simulation does not model a rater who knows the design.** If D's ratings track the
  model more closely than an outsider's would, the all-raters run inflates the gate and the
  consensus tau-b. That bias points toward "defaults stand", which is why the excluding-D
  run governs.
