## A1 — Direction-of-drift audit

Question: is S-58's "four labellers all drifted the same direction" true as stated?

Compute, per labeller (A, B, C, and the LLM extractor): count of 0.1 and of 0.9 across 36 cells.

Decision rule, fixed now:
- If the human raters and the extractor **under**-use 0.1 relative to 0.9, while the two
  labelling failures S-58 cites (the agent's 46-quote pass, the binary probe) erred **toward**
  0.1, then "same direction" is FALSE as written and S-58 must be restated as a claim about the
  *criterion* being applied (valence vs. posture), not about a shared direction of movement.
- If all four under-use or all four over-use 0.1, "same direction" stands.

## A2 — Global-impression test (STATE's analysis (i)), re-specified

Per labeller, decompose the 36 scores as grand mean + packet effect + dimension effect + residual.
Report eta-sq_packet = SS_packet / SS_total, with a within-labeller permutation null (10,000
shuffles of the 36 values).

Predictions, fixed now:
- **Global impression present** iff eta-sq_packet exceeds the permutation null at p < 0.05.
- If present, correlate the six packet effects between labeller pairs.
  - Packet effects **agree** across labellers -> the packet-level signal is shared. That is
    equally consistent with real company structure and with a shared reaction to packet tone;
    this analysis cannot separate those and will not claim to.
  - Packet effects **disagree** while each labeller individually shows a large packet effect ->
    labeller-specific global impression, which is what S-58 mechanism (a) needs.
- **Power warning, fixed now:** the packet-effect correlation is n = 6. Two-sided p < 0.05 needs
  |r| >= 0.81. Any |r| below that will be reported as uninformative and will NOT be used to
  support or reject a mechanism. eta-sq_packet, over 36 cells with a permutation null, is the
  only part of A2 that can carry inferential weight.

## A3 — Where the 0.1 band is actually used

Count 0.1 uses by dimension, pooled over A, B, C.
- Clusters on **autonomy** -> supports (a2): negative-sounding *evidence* is routed to the low
  band, and the band's own wording is what licenses it on the one dimension where that wording is
  pejorative.
- Uniformly rare, and rarest on autonomy -> supports (a1): the low band is read as a bad grade
  and avoided, most strongly where it sounds worst.
- (a1) and (a2) make opposite predictions here and are therefore separable on existing data.
  Mechanism (b), intensity conflation, predicts neither pattern: it predicts 0.1-vs-0.3 confusion
  spread evenly across dimensions regardless of connotation.

## A4 — Confidence as a scarcity check (not in STATE; free)

`confidence` in {0.1, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9}; 0.1 is defined in the frozen anchors as "almost nothing in
the packet bears on this dimension."

- Mean confidence per dimension, per rater.
- Spearman correlation between B-C band distance (the S-15 quantity) and the mean of the two
  raters' confidence on that cell, over 36 cells.

Predictions, fixed now:
- If feedback's mean confidence is the lowest of the six dimensions, the raters **knew** the
  evidence was thin. Their off-axis citations are then partly an artefact of an instrument with
  no abstain option, not proof that they believe supportiveness is feedback. This weakens
  mechanism (c) as an explanation of the Feedback result specifically.
- If feedback's confidence is at or above the six-dimension average, the raters believed they
  were on-axis, and (c) is genuine construct drift.
- If band distance correlates strongly negatively with confidence (rho <= -0.4), a material part
  of the S-15 disagreement is thin evidence rather than anchor ambiguity, and S-58 is **not**
  cleanly upstream of S-15.
- rho near 0 -> disagreement is independent of how much evidence the raters thought they had,
  which is the stronger case for S-58 being upstream.
