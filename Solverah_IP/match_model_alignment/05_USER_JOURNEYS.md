# Three fictional user journeys

**Status:** working draft, 2026-10-13. All three people are invented. Each walks your
loop: ARRIVE, UNDERSTAND, CLARIFY, GUIDE, CONTINUE. Steps marked **[CONCEPT]** depend on
the Reflection Engine or Understanding, which are not built; the rest uses what exists or
is being built this month.

---

## 1. Maya: performing well, not sustainable

**ARRIVE.** Maya, 31, is a product analyst at a fast-growing fintech. Her reviews are
excellent and she was promoted last spring. She comes to Solverah because she is "tired
in a way a holiday doesn't fix".

**UNDERSTAND.** Her core answers put her near the steady end of Pace. Her Environment
Report puts her job near the fast end. She rates the role a **4, "It's working well"**,
performance 5, and sustainability 2 ("could you keep working this way another year?").
The Situation Report shows Pace as straining, with her job faster than she prefers.

- Known: she performs well; her job runs faster than she prefers; she says the pace
  cannot last.
- Tentative [CONCEPT]: the cost of the pace is building up and is hidden by her results.
- Curious [CONCEPT]: is her steady preference lasting, or a reaction to this year?

**CLARIFY.** She sets her Pace tolerance to "a bit faster is fine, much faster is not"
and makes Pace a must-have. [CONCEPT] The Reflection Engine would ask what her best
working stretch looked like, to separate preference from exhaustion.

**GUIDE.** **Don't Act Yet** on leaving; **Prepare** instead. Guidance names the pace gap
and suggests a small experiment: one protected deep-work day a week for a month.

**CONTINUE.** Pulse check-ins track whether the experiment changes how sustainable the
job feels.

**What this journey shows.** By the primary outcome, Maya counts as **"doing well"**. Her
sustainability answer says otherwise. This is exactly why sustainability is an
exploratory outcome, and why a single "doing well" number would mislead here.

## 2. Daniel: mixed and unknown evidence, ending in Investigate

**ARRIVE.** Daniel, 38, an operations manager, has a final-round interview at a
regional logistics firm of about 2,000 people. He wants to know if it is the right move.

**UNDERSTAND.** He has made **Autonomy** a must-have: he left his last job over
micromanagement. He names the company and CompanyLens researches it in the background.

- Coverage: **based on 4 of 6 traits**.
- Pace and Collaboration: known, inside his tolerance.
- **Autonomy: mixed.** Recent reviews from corporate staff describe ownership and light
  approval. Recent reviews from warehouse sites describe tight procedures and sign-off
  for everything. The split is explained by **scope** (corporate versus sites), so both
  ends are shown with paraphrased evidence. The worst case breaks his must-have, so it
  is a **risk flag**, not a disqualification.
- **Feedback: unknown.** Too little evidence; no score, no penalty.

**CLARIFY.** He pastes the job posting. It says "own the regional process end to end",
which reads self-directed at role level. CompanyLens notes that the posting and the
site-level evidence disagree. The company stays in his list; nothing is hidden.

**GUIDE.** **Investigate.** Because Autonomy is a must-have and the evidence is split,
he gets interview questions, for example: "Walk me through the last process change on
this team. Who proposed it, and who had to approve it?" and "What decisions would I make
without checking with you first?"

**CONTINUE.** He takes the job and marks "I took this job". His 90-day Pulse check-in
asks how the role is working. That answer is a data point for whether CompanyLens's
mixed read was right.

## 3. Priya: changing circumstances and not enough evidence

**ARRIVE.** Priya, 27, a junior designer, is returning to work after six months caring
for a parent. She is not sure what she wants any more.

**UNDERSTAND.** Her core answers are inconsistent on Collaboration, so the
consistency check stores a low confidence for that trait (it is not shown to her). She sets wide tolerances everywhere and no must-haves.

- Known: she wants a steadier pace than her last job.
- Tentative [CONCEPT]: the pace preference may reflect her circumstances right now
  rather than a lasting need.
- Curious [CONCEPT]: whether her mixed Collaboration answers mean "it depends on the
  work" or "I haven't decided".

**CLARIFY.** She asks about a 40-person studio. CompanyLens finds evidence on only two
traits, so the answer is **"not enough evidence"**, not a match and not a 50/50 midpoint.

**GUIDE.** **Explore.** Guidance suggests two informational conversations with people
at studios she admires, focused on pace and how work is shared.

**CONTINUE.** She retakes the core instrument three months later. Her Collaboration
answers are now consistent. [CONCEPT] Understanding would keep her earlier answers as
context rather than overwrite them; today the newest answers drive the read.
