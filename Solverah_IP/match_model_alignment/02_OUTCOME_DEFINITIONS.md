# Outcome definitions

**Status:** working draft, 2026-10-13. The primary outcome is fixed by the
pre-registration of 2026-10-01 and is not changing. Everything else here is
**exploratory**: measured, reported with a label, never used to change the primary
analysis. Item wording for the new outcomes is a best-guess draft; the Situation Report
lane writes the final text and you revise it.

## Primary: "doing well"

- **Item (exact wording, Environment Report, asked last):** "Overall, how well is this
  role working for you?" Options: 1 "It isn’t working at all." 2 "It isn’t working
  well." 3 "A mixed picture." 4 "It’s working well." 5 "It’s working very well."
  People between jobs see the past-tense version ("how well was that role working for
  you?").
- **Definition:** "doing well" means a rating of 4 or 5 (ruling 2026-09-17).
- **Observed:** once per Environment Report, about the person's own current (or most
  recent) job. It is the only evaluative item in the form and comes last so it cannot
  colour the descriptive items.
- **Known biases:** single self-report; a current snapshot at whatever tenure the person
  happens to have, while your priors were elicited as "still doing well a year later";
  past-job answers are recalled, not observed. It is a measure of how the role is
  working, not of thriving or growth (your point).

## Exploratory outcomes

Each is a single optional 1 to 5 item asked **after** the primary item, mapped to 0 to 1
as (rating minus 1) divided by 4.

| Outcome | Definition | Draft item | Known biases |
|---|---|---|---|
| Satisfaction | How content the person is with the job overall | "How satisfied are you with this job?" | Same sitting as the primary item, so the two will correlate more than they truly do. |
| Performance | How well the person is doing the work, in their own view | "How well do you think you're performing in this role?" | Self-rated performance runs high and does not match manager ratings well. Treat as the person's view only. |
| Engagement | Energy and absorption in the work | "How energised are you by the work itself?" | Same sitting; mood on the day. |
| Sustainability | Whether the current way of working can continue | "Could you keep working the way you do now for another year?" | Same sitting; people under strain may still answer yes. This is the one that separates "performing" from "sustainable". |
| Retention (intention) | Whether the person expects to still be there | "How likely is it you'll still be in this role a year from now?" | Intention is not behaviour; job-market conditions move it. |

## Retention (observed), and why some outcomes need time

**Observed retention** is whether the person is actually still in the job. It is not
asked; it is seen:

1. A user marks "I took this job" on a company in CompanyLens. That creates a link between
   the person and that company.
2. Pulse check-ins attach to that link, starting with a check-in around 90 days.
3. Still checking in from the same job at 90 days, 6 months and a year is the observed
   retention signal. Leaving the link (or the platform) is ambiguous and is recorded as
   "unknown", not as "left".

**Which outcomes need longitudinal data:** observed retention, and any version of the
primary outcome that means "still doing well a year later". Both need the link and
repeated Pulse check-ins, so neither exists until people have taken jobs through
Solverah and stayed in contact. The others can be read from a single Environment
Report.

## The biases we are naming up front

- **Same sitting.** All exploratory items are answered together, right after the primary
  item. Their correlations with each other will be inflated. Comparisons between them are
  exploratory only.
- **Self-report performance.** Without a manager or objective measure, performance is the
  person's belief about their performance.
- **Who stays in contact.** People for whom a job is going badly may stop using Solverah,
  so later check-ins over-represent people who are doing fine.
- **The CompanyLens transfer gap.** Outcome rows pair a person with their own description
  of their job. CompanyLens pairs a person with a company described by researched evidence.
  A model that predicts well on the first is not yet shown to predict well on the second.
  The "I took this job" link is how we would test it.
